## What the ablations say about the point table
- The would-be contact set is entirely driven by the vulnerability component: with KEV and CVSS points removed none of the 107 stays (655 accounts change band). The vulnerability points come from banner-inferred CVEs (RAW-006). 84 of the 107 have a KEV host, 65 have CVSS band 3, 66 are in the placeholder US territory.
- Edge sensitivity of the vulnerability points: KEV 50 to 45 cuts would-be contacts from 107 to 63 (44 accounts change band). The threshold 40 and the KEV value are not independent; a KEV-only account (urgency 50) reaches 40 only with fit of at least 60 (50 x (0.5 + 0.5 x fit/100) = 40), and an urgency-60 account with no fit scores exactly 30.
- EOL (10) is a swing component: removing it drops 57 of 107; the exposed-and-outdated 30 drops 21; all exposure points together matter for 30 accounts. Cert (5, 12 accounts), legacy TLS (5, 8), SSH-only 5 (4) and sensitive stack (20, 3) barely move a band; they are nearly inert and can stay as ordering tie-breakers or be dropped without changing band sizes by more than a few accounts.
- Territory (placeholder US, 35 points) is the fit component that matters: removing it takes 56 accounts out of the would-be contact set (51 remain); declaring everyone in territory (the global case) adds 171 (278 would-be contacts) and changes 174 bands. The placeholder is shaping 56 of the 107 (52%), so the owner's territory answer is the biggest unknown for list size. Footprint is second (47 out): it is a size proxy that also favours hosting providers.
- The operator fit penalty (30) is not decisive: removing it adds 23 accounts to the would-be contact set (130) and removes none. 12 of the 13 flagged would-be contacts read as operators (step 5), and the penalty moves only 23 accounts; the adjudication gate does the real work.

## Proposals (the owner decides; none of this is a calibrated decision)
1. **Form**: keep (b), floor 0.5 (step 3). The forms differ in at most 2 of the top 107.
2. **Thresholds**. Evidence for 40 / 20 as the provisional pair:
   - X = 40. Scores 40 to 44 hold 57 accounts; 36 to 39 hold 48 (39: 11, 38: 17, 37: 13, 36: 7) and 35 holds 44 (a tie pile). There is no gap at 40. X = 45 gives 50 would-be contacts (0.86% of accounts), X = 35 gives 199, X = 30 gives 390. A move of 5 changes 57 (to 45) or 92 (to 35) accounts: tens, not hundreds, so 40 sits in a reasonably flat region. By name the list does not get cleaner with score: ORGANISATION is 9 of 50 in the top 50 and 13 of 57 in ranks 51 to 107; a read of 20 names at scores 35 to 39 (seed 23, review band, names only) gave 2 ORGANISATION, 8 OPERATOR, 10 UNSURE. Raising X buys little precision by name; it only shortens the list. Capacity should set X: the 107 would-be contacts exceed a 25 or 50 per week list and fall below 100 or 200 per week.
   - Y = 20 sits on a pile of 58 accounts at exactly 20; Y = 21 (499 review) or Y = 25 (466) are not stable because of the piles at 25 (110) and 30 (125). Reading 10 each side of a threshold on a pile is not meaningful. Prefer a count-capped review (top N by contact_score) over a score Y, as D9.7 already says for prod. For dev, Y = 20 gives 557 review-range accounts (664 including the held would-be contacts).
3. **Singleton cap**. All 24 singleton would-be contacts rest on one host; 23 have a KEV host and 23 are in the placeholder US territory. On names they read 9 organisation, 6 operator, 9 unsure, so singletons are as good as the rest by name. Under D10 every would-be contact is held, so the singleton cap changes nothing today; it will once the ASN reference or the adjudicator clears accounts. Proposal: keep it until the first adjudicated run, then measure the adjudicator's clearance rate for singletons separately; relax it, if at all, only for singletons with a KEV host and only if the owner accepts one-banner evidence (the KEV flag rests on a banner version). Relaxing adds at most 24 of 107 (22%) to the pool, so it cannot close a capacity gap.
4. **Operator flags**. Replace T2 by T2 without the `net` suffix branch (step 4). T1 plus T2 flags 12 of the 13 flagged would-be contacts correctly, but the flags catch only 25% of the operators I labelled; 75% carry no flagged word. More name rules will not close that gap (D7.2).
5. **Point table**. The data supports only coarse steps (19 distinct urgency values, four bulk values). KEV 50 against CVSS-3 40 is the one choice that moves the list (44 accounts per 5 points); both come from banner inference, so the owner should confirm it. The sensitive stack and SSH-only points are inert.
6. **DQ-013 (skip band)**. Of the 659 skip accounts with urgency above 0, the maximum contact_score is 19; none has urgency of 50 or more, and none has a KEV host. The 42 with a non-SSH admin or database port and the 32 with CVSS 7.0 or more sit just under the review threshold (scores 19: 5 accounts, 18: 1, 15: 3, 14: 8, 13: 18, listing only those with a risky non-SSH port, CVSS 7 or KEV). So skip is not "safe"; "Exposure is limited." reads as a low-risk judgement for accounts the score ranks one step below review. 295 of the 659 have only certificate or legacy-TLS points. Proposal for the owner and senior engineer: change the wording to a statement about the score ("Below the review threshold") and consider routing accounts with a non-SSH admin port or CVSS 7 or more into review whatever their score (at most about 70, overlap between the 42 and the 32 not measured).
7. **Expectation for contacts after adjudication**. Pool: 107 would-be contacts at 40 (1.85% of 5,790). My name read: 22 ORGANISATION, 48 OPERATOR, 37 UNSURE. If the adjudicator clears every ORGANISATION and between none and all of the UNSURE, the cleared list is 22 to 59 (a central guess of 25 to 40; not an interval). It may also promote some of the 557 review-range accounts: a read of 20 at 35 to 39 gave 10% organisation, so a further few tens, unmeasured. A fair expectation on the sample is a few tens, roughly 20 to 100 accounts, not a hundred-plus. That fits 25 per week, is marginal at 50 and does not fit 100 or 200 per week. The full file is not measured and cannot be scaled from 5,790 companies per 100,000 rows (D9.8). The adjudicator can fail in the way the reviewer does, so this is a range of plausible outcomes, not a promise.
8. **What the owner must supply to firm it up**: rep capacity (per rep per week, number of reps), the territory list (the placeholder drives 56 of the 107), the segment answers in icp.md (are universities, public sector, insurers, hosting customers wanted), the operator-type reference keyed on ASN (ASdb or PeeringDB), the LLM ceilings, a decision on security vendors (Comodo Group is among the would-be contacts although D2 excludes security vendors), and a verdict on duplicate accounts (PDR twice, Leaseweb three times).
