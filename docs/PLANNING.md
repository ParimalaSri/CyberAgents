# CyberIntel planning

Owner: senior-data-engineer. Status: draft for owner review, 2026-10-11. Design detail is in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`.

## The user
A sales rep at a cybersecurity software company with **ten minutes** before they start calling. They want to open
a list, see the best accounts first, read one sentence on why each is there, and copy a first email. They will not
read a methodology, decode a score, or trust a list that cannot explain itself.

## Use cases chosen, and why

| # | use case | why this one |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rank accounts by need** (score, band: contact / review / skip / exclude) | It answers the brief's core question, "which businesses need our services right now". A ranked list is the form a rep can act on. |
| 2 | **Explain each account in plain English** (`why`) | A score nobody can explain is not used. The `why` is built from the same signals as the score, so it is always true to the data. |
| 3 | **Draft the first touch** for top accounts | It answers "how do we reach them first". It is the best-fit use of a writing model, and the draft is grounded in the account's own signals. |
| 4 | **Judge the ambiguous middle with an LLM** (review band only) | Rules handle clear cases cheaply. The middle needs judgement. It is also the one place where evals, tracing and cost control can be shown on a small, defensible volume. |
| 5 | **Show what the data cannot say** (share of unattributable infrastructure, unresolved rate) | Trust. About 87% to 90% of scan rows in the head sample belong to cloud, CDN and hosting owners, not customers (measured on the spread sample at stage 2: 86.38% of rows cannot be attributed to a customer, 13.62% can). Saying so openly is part of the product. |

Why not other ideas: a market map, a technology-stack search tool and a generic alerting feed are filters on the
dataset, not decisions for a rep. Contact discovery and CRM sync are out of reach of this data (below).

## What informed the ICP, signals and segmentation
This is general B2B practice as I know it, not a literature review. I have **not** cited sources and have not
checked any link.
- **ICP and fit.** Sales teams define an ideal customer profile by firmographics (industry, size, geography) and by
  environment (what they run, what they must protect), then filter territories to the reps who cover them. Fit says
  "is this our buyer".
- **Buying signals and timing.** Teams prioritise accounts by signals of need or trigger events, separate from fit.
  In security, the usual need signals are exposure, known-exploited flaws, unsupported software and hygiene failures
  (expired certificates, legacy protocols). Urgency says "do they need help now".
- **Scoring in two parts.** Fit and urgency are scored separately and combined, so a great-fit company with no sign
  of need does not outrank a poor-fit company in distress, and the reason can be read off the parts.
- **Bands and capacity.** Accounts are bucketed (work now, review, not now, excluded) and the top band is sized to
  what the team can actually work in a week. This is why thresholds are calibrated on the real distribution, not copied.
- **Precision over breadth.** A short list a rep trusts beats a long one they ignore. This drives the choice to
  group rows into companies conservatively and to report what is left unresolved.
- **First touch.** Short, specific, factual messages that name one observation and ask one question. No fear or
  breach claims.

What the data adds beyond filters: combined signals (a reachable admin service that is also on unsupported
software), a fit-then-urgency structure, and an explicit exclusion of the infrastructure that makes up most of the dump.
The signals are in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` section 3; none is approved yet.

## Out of scope (explicit)
- Finding people or contact details; email sending; CRM integration. The dump has no contacts.
- Company size, revenue or sector enrichment from outside the dataset (allowed by the brief, deferred).
- Any recency or trend signal: the dump covers about 61 minutes of one day.
- Naming the owners of vulnerable cloud-hosted estates (needs tenant data we do not have).
- Competitor or channel-partner lists (D2).
- A multi-user app, logins, saved lists, feedback loops from real sales outcomes.
- Live LLM calls on every page view; drafts are pre-generated by default.

## Success measures
Measured where possible on the owner's labels; targets are for the owner to set because none are known yet.

| measure | how measured | target |
|---|---|---|
| Review-band precision and recall for `contact` | `evals/run_eval.py` on 20 to 30 owner-labelled accounts, with the previous prompt and a rules-only baseline | beat the rules-only baseline; targets to be set by the owner |
| Every account explainable | contract check: band set and `why` non-empty on all rows | 100% |
| Data integrity | DQ PASS on each layer: `companies <= hosts`, signals rows == companies rows, no denylist names, scores 0 to 100 | PASS, full dataset before prod |
| Unresolved share reported | stage report and app footer show the infrastructure share | always shown |
| Cost within ceiling | `cost-report` from `traces/`; ceiling enforced in `llm/client.py` | at or under the ceiling set by the owner |
| Rep usability | `sales-team` feedback (usability only, never ground truth) and the owner's own read: can the top 10 accounts be understood and one email copied in ten minutes | owner judgement |
| Deliverables | hosted app, repo, planning and architecture docs, half-to-one page reflection | see traceability table in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` section 12 |
